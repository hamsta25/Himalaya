### Standardized Test Scores

**GRE (Graduate Record Examination):**
- Generally required for most programs
- Competitive scores expected
- Quantitative: High scores essential for engineering (typically 160+/170)
- Verbal: Moderate to high scores (typically 150+/170)
- Analytical Writing: 4.0+/6.0
- No specific cutoffs, but competitive applicants score high

**English Proficiency (TOEFL/IELTS):**
- Required for non-native English speakers
- May be waived with sufficient English-medium education
- TOEFL: Typically 90+ (iBT), competitive scores 100+
- IELTS: Typically 7.0+, competitive scores 7.5+

**What This Means for Hamza:**
- ⚠️ GRE: Need to schedule and prepare
- 🟡 English: 7+ years in Italian universities (English-taught courses) - may qualify for waiver
- 📋 Verify specific program requirements

## Application Components - Detailed Expectations

### 1. Online Application Form

**MIT Expects:**
- Complete and accurate information
- All fields filled appropriately
- Consistent information across documents
- Professional email address
- Updated contact information
- Application fee payment

### 2. Transcripts

**MIT Expects:**
- Official transcripts from all institutions
- Sealed and sent directly from institution
- English translation if needed
- Grade scale explanation if different from US
- All courses and grades visible
- Degree conferral dates

**Format Requirements:**
- Official seal/stamp
- Registrar signature
- Unopened envelope (if mailed)
- Electronic transcripts from official source

### 3. Test Scores

**MIT Expects:**
- Official scores sent directly from testing agency
- Scores from within validity period (typically 5 years)
- All sections completed
- Competitive scores for program
- Scores received by deadline

### 4. Letters of Recommendation

**MIT Expects:**
- Submitted through official MIT portal
- Written specifically for this application
- From qualified recommenders
- Detailed and specific
- Professional format
- Submitted by deadline

### 5. CV/Resume

**MIT Expects:**
- Comprehensive but concise (typically 2 pages)
- Academic and professional experience
- Research and publications
- Technical skills
- Awards and honors
- Professional format
- No exaggeration

### 6. Statement of Purpose

**MIT Expects:**
- Well-written and proofread
- Specific to MIT and program
- Clear research/career goals
- Evidence of preparation
- Authentic voice
- Within word limit
- No plagiarism

### 7. Supplemental Materials (if applicable)

**MIT May Accept:**
- Research papers or publications
- Portfolio of work (for some programs)
- Additional writing samples
- Relevant certifications
- Documentation of achievements

**MIT Generally Does Not Want:**
- Unsolicited recommendation letters beyond required
- Lengthy attachments
- Creative presentations (unless specifically requested)
- Materials not directly relevant to academic/professional qualifications

## Timeline Expectations

### Application Deadlines

**Typical Timeline:**
- Fall admission: Applications due December-January
- Spring admission: Applications due September-October (if offered)
- Recommendation letters: Usually same as application
- Test scores: Usually same as or slightly before application

**What This Means for Hamza:**
- 📋 Need to confirm exact deadline for target program
- ⚠️ January trip may conflict with peak preparation time
- 🎯 Should aim to complete most work by early January
- 📋 Set personal deadlines 1-2 weeks before MIT deadlines

### Response Timeline

**After Submission:**
- Acknowledgment: Within 1-2 weeks
- Review period: 2-4 months typically
- Interview requests: If applicable, during review period
- Decisions: Typically March-April for fall admission
- Acceptance deadline: Usually April 15 (for fall)
